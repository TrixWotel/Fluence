# Fluence

My personal web playground, live at https://trixwotel.github.io/Fluence/

## Branches

Every branch is one part of the site. A push to any of them puts the whole site back together and deploys it. The workflow that does that is .github/workflows/pages.yml on this branch, and every other branch's copy of that file only asks this one to deploy, since this is the branch the site's deploy rules allow. A new subject needs adding to that file here, and nowhere else.

* **space** is served at the top: the home page, Schedule (schedule/), Modules (study/), Board and Station (board/ and station/, two of the modules, with journal/ going on to Station), Music (music/), Data, Code and Notes (data/, code/ and notes/, one app kept in workspace/) and Settings (settings/). It also holds settings.js (see below) and kit/: the look and the bar every page shares, the music player every page carries (kit/music/), and the test runner and question toolkit that the Math, Science, Physics, Spanish and History tests share, with the tests like the ones in class (kit/exam-kit.js makes and marks them, kit/exam.js shows them).
* **geography** is served at /geography: find countries or capitals on the world map, or one continent at a time.
* **math** is served at /math: Triangle Angles (220 variants), AMC 10 Practice (1,884 variants, 157 topics, from problem 1 hard to problem 25 hard) and AMC 12 Practice (1,704 variants, 142 topics: 24 that only the AMC 12 tests, and the AMC 10's harder ones moved down the paper).
* **science** is served at /science: Heat and Energy tests like the ones in class, and Heat Capacity practice with Q = mcΔT (220 variants).
* **physics** is served at /physics: Kinematics with motion graphs (280 variants), Motion in a Straight Line (240), Vectors (240) and Projectile Motion (260), each with tests like the ones in class too.
* **spanish** is served at /spanish: Vocabulary (1,100 words in 25 themes, 2,200 variants), the Preterite (270 variants) and the Imperfect (180 variants).
* **history** is served at /history: each chapter is a story where you make the choices, with things to try and a quick check. It has The Middle Ages and The Wars of Religion, each with notes, tests like the ones in class (key words and dates, a source, an answer to explain) and practice asked twelve ways, such as myth or true, who am I, sorting, timelines and matching (380 variants in all).
* **english** is served at /english: How essays work (14 short steps, each with one idea, things to try and something to write yourself), and a room for writing essays, on a prompt or your own question from class: plan it, write it (saved as you type), have the page check what can be counted, mark what can't with a rubric, and compare with a model. Rules in the browser, with no AI and nothing sent anywhere.

There is no build step. The files are served as they are.

## The pages

Every page has the same bar at the top: Home, Modules and Music (and Data, Code and Notes, when they are on), Search, and Settings. When a song is playing, the music player is at the bottom of every page too.

* **Home** is an assistant. It looks at what you've been doing in this browser (the topic you left off on, a test half done, what you missed on your last test, what keeps tripping you up, what's playing, how long you've been at it, the time of day), says what it notices, and offers the three things that would help most, with Something else for more. You can also tell it what you want, like “quiz me”, “play something”, “test me on AMC 12” or “how am I doing?”. It works by rules written in kit/assistant.js, with nothing sent anywhere, so it isn't a chatbot. Settings → Home → Layout has the tiles instead: a greeting for the time of day or a holiday, a line that changes with the date, the time, the month, your music and what you’ve done, and a tile for each part: Music (what’s playing, with its controls and what’s up next, or your newest songs to start one), Modules (Continue, with the topic you were learning and how far along that test you are), Your progress (topics learned, days in a row, best scores, and each subject), Today (the date, how far through the year, and the next holidays and days off), a Question of the day from a different subject each day, answered right there, and Quick (a column of buttons from settings.js). Recent and Data show there too when Data, Code and Notes are on. It fits one screen, in the middle of the window, with no scrolling (Settings → Home → Text puts its words and buttons on the left, in the middle or on the right): three tiles in a row (two on a smaller screen, one on a phone), each as tall as what's in it, and the others as buttons under them that bring one in. Auto picks the three by what you’ve been doing: the song that’s playing, the topic you learned last, whether today’s question is answered, your progress, and the time of day (music counts more in the evening, study in the morning). At night the welcome is a big clock with quieter colors, and in the evening music can take two places. Settings → Home can keep Classic (your tiles in their order), Study first, Music first or Calm instead.
* **Schedule** (schedule/) is your plan as a calendar, a space of its own in the bar, laid out like Music: tabs at the top (Today, Week, Classes, Deadlines, To do and Pages), a view's name big under them, and nothing boxed in. Its pages are yours, kept with your progress and never part of the site: Week has your days, a section for each day or set of days (## Monday, ## Sunday to Thursday nights) and a line for each thing with its time (4:30 Homework · 90 min). A line with minutes is something you check off, and one with neither minutes nor an end is there to see, like getting up. After a · a line can also give a link, the days it's for, from or until a date, odd or even weeks, or first Saturday, and a line like that takes the place of the usual one at its time. A section like ## On game days, ## In exam weeks or ## On trip days, only is a switch that changes those days. A line with a time it ends and no minutes (4:00–5:00 Lesson, 7:00–7:30 Dinner) is something set, like a lesson or a meal. Classes has your school's days, ## Day 1, ## Day 2 and on, with a line for each class (9:00–9:50 Biology · Room 204). The Days go with the weekdays (Monday is Day 1 or Day 6) and the weeks take turns, from what ## Rotation says: Schedule asks once, and a Day's label in the calendar puts it right. ## No school has the days off and ## Colors a color for a class. On a school day the classes take the place of the week's School, something with no time named like a class (Lab: …) goes at that class's time, and a switch named like one (## On lab days) turns on by itself on its days. Deadlines has your dates (under a heading for each year), Sleep a table of when it's lights out from when, and How to study a section for each kind of block, which Schedule and Home quote. Week lays the week out by the hour, Sunday to Saturday (a day at a time on a phone), sized to fit the window down to the music player so all of it shows with nothing to scroll (on a very short window the page scrolls as a whole), its dates and the way to the weeks around it beside the tabs. On it: the classes in their colors, your own blocks, and what has set times (a lesson, School, and quieter, a meal or the walk home, unless Colors gives it a color), deadlines at the top of their day and a line for now in the theme's color. What has set times crosses itself off once it's over; what's yours, you check off. Free time, the night and anything under a quarter of an hour stay off it, and until Classes says which Day it is, School asks. A click on a block shows when and where, how to study it, and Start and Check it off; ‹ and › (or ← and →) go a week back and on. Today says what's on now and how long it has left, with a way to start it, how today's going and tonight's lights out, then lists the day in order, a row each with a circle to check it off (the night as one row), then the switches and, in the evening, tomorrow. Pages lists the rest of your pages with their first lines. Classes shows each week of Days the way schools do, Deadlines what's coming with a check for each (it ticks the line on the page), and Sleep tonight's row lit. Every page is Markdown you can change (Edit; what you type is kept as you go), and New page adds one. A day counts at 70%, and Today shows how many days in a row. A day runs until 5 in the morning, so a late night still counts as that day. With the Assistant layout, Home says the plan first too: what's on now, what went by without a check, and once the night starts, lights out, and past it, bed. It lists today (school as one row, the class that's on now), and you can tell it things, like “did java”, “it's a game day” or “what now”. The bar on every page shows what you're due to be doing and how long it has left, or at night, lights out. The pages are in fluentio.schedule and what you check off in fluentio.plan, both saved with your progress, so every browser has the same plan; where two browsers changed the same page, the newer is kept. Settings → Home can leave the plan off Home or the bar.
* **Your progress** (topics learned, best scores, tests under way, days in a row) is saved by itself in the repo Music is connected to, on its progress branch as progress.json, so any browser you connect carries on from the same place. Settings → Your data shows how the last save went. Without a connection it stays in the browser.
* **Music** plays your own songs, kept in your private Database repo on GitHub (its music branch: songs/ in a folder for each artist, covers/ and music.json). Drop songs on the page to add them (their title, artist, album and cover come from their tags, and a song already in your library is skipped), then play them by song, album, artist, playlist or what you liked, with shuffle, repeat and a queue. Now Playing shows the cover big with a ring of bars that moves with the music, and the page takes its colors from the cover. The player stays at the bottom of every page, and the sound doesn't stop as you go from page to page: the next page opens out of sight in a frame, and once it's fully drawn, covers and all, it crosses over with the last one while the top bar and the player stay still. The page you first opened stays underneath holding the song. Songs are kept in the browser once played, so they start at once. Media keys and the lock screen work too. Coming back to Music brings you to the same tab, album, playlist or search, scrolled to the same place. A second copy of a song that gets in some other way goes by itself when Music opens: the biggest file stays, with the likes and playlists of the others. It connects with the token Data, Code and Notes use, or one of its own.
* **Modules** lists the subjects, with what you've learned and your best scores in each.
* **Station** (station/) is your notes and journal, in folders, and one of the modules. A note is Markdown, and shows laid out: click any part of it to change that part as Markdown, so only what you're working on shows its marks. [[A note's name]] links to it (type [[ and your notes come up to pick from), ![[a picture.png]] shows a picture, and under every note are the notes that link to it, each with the line the link is on. Tables change cell by cell, like a sheet: Tab and Enter move on, and a new row or column is one click. A / at the start of a line offers a heading, a list, a checklist, a table, a table comparing the notes in a folder, a picture and more. Today opens today's journal entry (journal/ has a file for each day, named by its date), and the days written in Journal before it was part of Station are there too. A picture you paste or drop goes in the nearest images folder, a web address you paste goes in your sources note with the day (Settings → Station can turn that off), and a PDF stays on this computer and never goes to GitHub, so what you keep there is your own words and pictures. Start a research project makes a folder for the things you compare with a note for each, a table for each part you compare them on, and notes for parts and sources. Everything is saved in the browser as you type, and a few seconds later in the repo Music is connected to, on its notes branch, as Markdown files in their folders. So the branch opens in Obsidian too, and a note changed there comes back here. A note changed in two places at once keeps both versions.
* **Board** is an endless whiteboard for ideas, flowcharts and diagrams, and one of the modules (board/): a wide frame under its heading, with a button in its corner that makes it as big as the window (board/?big opens it that way). It has one small toolbar: select, pen, box, diamond, circle, arrow and text. Arrows stay joined to the shapes they connect, and can be straight, curved or at right angles. One row of styles shows when there's something to apply them to: five colors, a fill, the line's thickness, dashes, and text's size and code font. What you select and drag shows in the theme's color. A keyboard's undo and redo work as usual, and a touch screen gets buttons for them. Settings → Board has the rest: 10 fonts, which tools show (line, eraser and moving around too), the right-click menu, all of Excalidraw's own tools, and buttons to show all your drawings, zoom to 100%, save as a picture or clear the board. Excalidraw, which loads from the web, does the drawing underneath. Every change is saved in the browser straight away and in your private Database repo a few seconds later, on its station branch (named from when the board was called Station): station.excalidraw, which opens on excalidraw.com too, with each picture as a file of its own in files/. Another browser connected to the same repo opens the same board, and two browsers' drawings are put together rather than saved over each other. Without a connection it stays in the browser.
* **Data**, **Code** and **Notes** are switched off for now (SPACES_ON in kit/look.js): their code stays, but they aren't in the bar, on the home page, in Search or in Settings, their pages go to the home page, and only that line can turn them back on. When on, they are where your own things live, each a space of its own in the bar (one app, in workspace/). **Notes** keeps Markdown notes in folders, where [[a note's name]] links to another note and the right side shows the outline and the links both ways. **Data** holds databases (one can hold more), each with its docs and sheets. A doc is your own writing, with a Google Docs, Slides or Sheets link to open beside it, and a sheet takes CSV in and out and shows each column's numbers. **Code** keeps files in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python, Java and C++, in folders too, with their words in color. JavaScript and Python run right there (Python with Pyodide, which downloads the first time it runs), an HTML page shows in a preview with the CSS and JavaScript files it links, and Java and C++ download to run on a computer. Everything shows as cards, folders and databases first: drag a card onto a folder to move it, or drop files from your computer to add them. Each space's View menu sorts its cards and turns its own animations off. All of it stays in your browser, never in this repo. Settings → GitHub can also save it to a private repo of yours (TrixWotel/Database) as files laid out like its folders, so every browser and Fluent workspace connected to it shows the same things, and Settings downloads a backup too. A link from when they were the Workspace goes on to where that thing is now.
* **Settings** picks a theme (Fluence, Fluent with the Fluent browser's own pure black look, or GitHub with GitHub's colors) or changes each part of the look: the colors (or a ready-made pair), the color of the logo on the browser's tab, light or dark, the style, font, corners, motion (with Data, Code and Notes each on their own), the home page's tiles and buttons, and the names. Each change shows straight away and stays in that browser. Copy as settings.js turns them into a new settings.js to paste here for every device. Settings also downloads and restores a backup of everything Fluence keeps in the browser, and connects Data, Code and Notes to a private GitHub repo (a token that can only read and write that repo stays in the browser, never in a backup).

Keys: ⌃K (or /) searches everything, and ⌃1 to ⌃3 go to Home, Modules and Music, with ⌃4 to ⌃6 for Data, Code and Notes when they're on (on Windows and Linux, Alt with the number). Settings → Keys can change each one: click it and press the new key.

## Settings

**settings.js**, at the top of the space branch, holds the colors and settings for the whole site. To change one on GitHub, open the file, press the pencil, change a value and press Commit changes. The site follows a minute or two later. (Or try changes in Settings on the site first, then paste its Copy as settings.js here.)

* **name** and **tagline** are the words at the top of the home page, and **greeting** turns the home page’s welcome (a greeting, and a line that changes with the time, the date, holidays, your music and what you’ve done) on or off. **homeLayout** is 'assistant', or for tiles 'auto' (it changes with the time of day), or always 'classic', 'study', 'music' or 'calm'. **homeAlign** is where the home page's words and buttons sit: 'left', 'center' or 'right' (the page itself stays in the middle of the window). **planHome** and **planBar** (true or false) show your plan from Schedule on Home and in the bar.
* **logoColor** is the color of the icon on the browser's tab: any color, or '' for the usual blue and violet. The pages themselves don't show it.
* **theme** is 'auto' (the device's light or dark mode), 'light' or 'dark'.
* **colors** has a light set and a dark set: the accent (buttons, links and anything selected), a second color for gradients, the background, cards, text, quieter text, borders, right and wrong answers, and the second line on graphs. Any CSS color works, like '#2f6fed' or 'tomato'.
* **map** is the colors of the Geography map, light and dark.
* **font** is 'system', 'rounded', 'serif', 'mono' or any CSS font list, and **corners** is how round the cards and buttons are, in pixels.
* **style** is 'glow' (soft shadows, glowing edges and gradients) or 'flat' (borders and solid colors, like GitHub).
* **textSize** is how big the words are, in pixels (12 to 20).
* **keys** are the keys for search, home, study (Modules), music, schedule, data, code and notes, written like 'Ctrl+K' (Ctrl, Alt, Shift or Meta with a key, Meta being ⌘ on a Mac). An empty one ('') turns that key off.
* **animations** turns every animation on or off, **speed** makes them faster (2) or slower (0.5), and **floatingColors** turns the soft colors drifting behind the pages on or off. **animationsIn** turns the animations in Data, Code and Notes on or off, each on its own. A device set to reduce motion never gets the animations.
* **home** is the home page's tiles in order ('workspace' for Recent, 'music', 'study', 'progress', 'today', 'question' for the question of the day, 'data' and 'quick'), and **quick** the Quick tile's buttons, each a title and a link.
* **showDaysOff** turns your days off on the Today tile on or off, and **daysOff** is the list: a name, an emoji, the first day and (if it's more than one) the last. **days-off.ics**, next to settings.js, is the same list as a calendar link. Fluence and Fluent's Calendar stay separate unless you add that link in Fluent's Settings → Calendar → Other Calendars.
* **boardTitle** is Board's name, **boardFont** its font, **boardTools** the tools on its toolbar, **boardMenu** whether a right-click brings up a menu, and **boardFull** whether it shows all of Excalidraw's own tools. **stationTitle** is Station's name, and **stationSources** whether a web address you paste into a note goes into your sources note too. **musicTitle** is Music's name, and **scheduleTitle** Schedule's. **dataTitle**, **codeTitle** and **notesTitle** are the names of Data, Code and Notes. **studyTitle**, **studyIntro** and **study** are Modules (the study/ page): its title, the line under it, and the subjects in order, each with an emoji, its words, a link and its own color.

A typo can't break the site. Anything missing or mistyped falls back to the usual look.

## How it looks and moves

kit/look.js puts settings.js (and what Settings changed in that browser) on each page before the page is drawn, and kit/bar.js adds the bar, Search and the keys. kit/site.css is the look every page shares: the colors drifting behind the page (they carry on from one page to the next), cards that lift and glow under the pointer, buttons, page transitions, and the animations the pages share. kit/tests.css is the test pages' own layout, where questions rise in, a right choice bumps and a wrong one shakes, hints fade in a sentence at a time, and graphs draw themselves.

## The tests

Each question is built from its variant number, so variant 137 is always the same question with the same picture. Every test has these ways in:

* **A test like the ones in class** (Science, Physics and History) is a paper of three or four longer questions, each built on a context such as a lab, a data set, a graph or a source to read, with lettered parts and their marks. Some parts are calculations in steps, so a slip costs only that step. Others pick the variables, choose and explain why, explain with particles or energy, sketch a graph, fill in a table of processed data, or choose what to plot so a model gives a straight line, plot it, drag a line of best fit and use its gradient (an answer that follows from your own line earns the marks too). The page marks the numbers, choices, tables and graphs, and you mark your written answers and sketches against the mark scheme by ticking the points they make, with a model answer and what loses marks beside them. Each test is built from its number, so Test 37 is always the same test. Your answers are kept in the browser as you go, so you can leave and come back, and your best score stays there. The questions are original, in the style of real class tests.
* **Practice** (called a test on the Math and Spanish pages) picks random variants spread over every kind of question, in a random order every time. It has its usual length (25 for the AMC, 10 for the others) unless you choose another, and your best score on a full-length test stays in your browser. An AMC test comes in three kinds: like the real AMC 10 (easier problems first, so question 1 is about as hard as problem 1 and the last as hard as problem 25), harder problems only (like numbers 16 to 25), or mixed (every level in a random order), each with its own best score. Afterwards the score page shows how each unit went and what to work on, each topic a link to its lesson.
* **Learning** follows a path: the topics in units, in an order where each builds on the ones before, so Continue always takes you to the next one. Each topic is a short lesson (how to spot it, the idea, how to do it, the facts worth knowing by heart, what to watch out for), a worked example shown a step at a time, then practice with no clock and as many tries as you need. The first hint, like a wrong answer, gives the lesson's tip (or one written for that kind of question), and the next hints show the working a step at a time. A topic is learned after 3 right in a row on the first try without hints. Progress is saved in your browser unless you turn that off.
* **Ways to answer:** choose one of the choices, type a number or a word, put things in order (tap them in turn), match each one to what it is (a menu for each), or choose all that fit. History uses all of them.
* **One question** opens any variant by its number, with a worked solution (on the Math and Spanish pages it’s called Practice).

Links can go straight to one: math/#amc10/test starts an AMC test (math/#amc10/test/40 one of 40 problems, math/#amc10/test/hard the harder problems), science/#heat/exam starts a test like the ones in class (science/#heat/exam/37 opens Test 37), physics/#kinematics/practice starts practice, physics/#kinematics/learn shows the learning path, and physics/#kinematics/137 opens variant 137.

The AMC 10 and AMC 12 problems are original problems in the style of the contests. Real AMC problems are the MAA's copyright, so none are copied here.
